### The component STL exporter — SHIPPED 🖨, and you're right that nobody else has it

Select a printable component and the property panel now has a
**🖨 STL for printing (mm)** button. Unlike the display shell, these are
**true watertight solids** built for manufacturing:

| Component | What you get |
|---|---|
| Nose cone | Hollow shell at your wall thickness (or solid if "filled"), **including the shoulder** and its end cap if enabled — the exact kernel profile for all six shapes + parameter |
| Transition | Same, with **both** fore and aft shoulders |
| Fins (trapezoid / elliptical / freeform) | **One fin** as a flat prism at your thickness, **with the through-the-wall tab merged in** |
| Centering ring | True annulus: OD from the parent tube's bore, ID from the mount tube |
| Bulkhead | Disc sized to the parent tube's bore |
| Body / inner tube, coupler, engine block, launch lug, tube fin | Proper hollow tube at its real wall |

Every generated solid is verified **manifold** (every edge shared by exactly
two faces, outward normals, positive volume) by the test suite — the meshes
are proof-checked, not just eyeballed. The geometry went through an
adversarial verification pass that caught (and fixed, with pinning tests) two
real defects before release: a transition's *fore* shoulder cap was silently
dropped (an 11% volume error on capped reducers), and a freeform outline
whose last point duplicated its first broke watertightness when a tab was
merged. Live-verified on the built app: your Wild Child's nose exported at
**exactly 260.35 mm** (221.23 nose + 39.12 shoulder) with the right 40.64 mm
base diameter. Millimetres, so slicers import at the
right size with zero fiddling. Honest limitations, printed in the button
tooltip: fins export as flat prisms (cross-section/airfoil shaping is left to
sanding or your CAM); cant angle isn't baked; always check the fit before a
long print. The camera shroud is NOT in this list yet — its printable form is
a real 3D surface (not a revolve/extrude), and you design yours in Fusion
anyway; say the word if you want a simple extruded approximation.

### STEP / CNC — my recommendation (your question)

You're right that STL is wrong for subtractive work, and right that **STEP is
the universal answer** for Fusion 360/SolidWorks. My recommendation is a
**two-step path**:

1. **Now / next session — DXF profiles for flat parts.** The majority of
   CNC'd rocket parts are flat plate: fins, centering rings, bulkheads,
   avionics sleds. For 2D profiling (router/laser/waterjet/plasma — and
   Fusion sketch import), the lingua franca is **DXF**, and a DXF outline
   exporter is small, dependency-free, and exact (the same outlines the STL
   prisms use). This covers most real CNC use for a fraction of the cost of
   STEP.
2. **Phase 2 — real STEP via OpenCascade compiled to WebAssembly**
   (opencascade.js). It's the only credible way to write true B-rep STEP in
   a browser with no backend. Cost: a ~10–30 MB WASM chunk — lazy-loaded
   only when someone clicks STEP export, never part of the normal app or the
   offline precache. Payoff: exact revolved solids (the nose curve as a real
   spline surface, not triangles) that open as editable bodies in Fusion and
   SolidWorks. This is its own build (roughly a session), and validation has
   to be yours — you have the Fusion + SolidWorks seats; I'd hand you test
   files and you tell me what imports cleanly.

If you confirm, I'll do DXF in the next batch and plan the STEP build after.
Agreed on skipping PDF until users ask.

tracker for the site AND all the tools**, not two trackers (site-feedback +
tools-feedback). Reason: the site/tool boundary is obvious to you and
invisible to users — "the Black Powder Calculator page looks broken on my

- Routing = a required **"Where did this happen?" dropdown** in the issue

  (7 steps, ~10 min), README.md, two issue forms (bug/feature — the bug form

  your four tools; adding a fifth is a 3-line change.
- If you'd rather keep site-feedback site-only, the same kit works verbatim
